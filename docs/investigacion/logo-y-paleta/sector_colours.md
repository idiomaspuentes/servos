# Sector brand and app-icon colours (Bible translation, Bible engagement, language tools)

Research date: 17 October 2026. **How the hex values were obtained** (applies to every row below):
- **"site CSS"**: the value is a colour variable defined in the organisation's own live website code (for example `--brand-red`, Elementor "global colours", WordPress presets, `theme-color`, web-app manifest). That is official, but it is not a published brand guide. Values that look like template defaults are flagged.
- **"SVG fill"**: the colour is written into the official logo SVG file.
- **"approx."**: the value was measured from pixels with a median-cut quantiser and a background-ring sample. The images were official App Store icons (iTunes Search API `artworkUrl512`), Google Play icons (`og:image`), and favicons or logos downloaded from the official site (or, for sites that block bots, through Google's favicon service). Anti-aliasing and gradients shift these values by a few units.
- Family names are my own classification by hue and lightness.

## 1. Brand colours of the named organisations and apps (current identities, 2023–2026)

### Takeaway
Of about 39 identities checked, about 13 sit in blue/navy/cyan and about 10 in red/crimson/burgundy. Together that is roughly 60% of the sector. Orange/gold is the default *secondary accent* (about 9 organisations). Lime/yellow-green clusters among translation *tools* (Paratext, ETEN, Door43, illumiNations accent). Purple, deep forest green, terracotta, plum and ink black are rare or absent as *primary* colours. Deep teal is no longer open, because Wycliffe USA moved into it in 2025.

### Inferences
- **Servos's current palette is almost unfoldingWord's palette.** Ink `#17496a` vs unfoldingWord navy `#014263`; ochre `#d39a2c` vs unfoldingWord button orange `#e79e36`. The paper `#f4efe4` is nearly identical to FCBH's `#f4eede`. As it stands, Servos reads as an unfoldingWord sub-brand, not as its own identity. That is acceptable if family resemblance is the goal; it does not give Servos a distinct identity.
- The dark blue / blue-teal cluster that Servos's ink sits in is the busiest corner of the sector: unfoldingWord `#014263`, Wycliffe USA `#004F59`, LBT `#0f597d`, SIL navy `#003049`, FCBH deep blue `#003c5b`, DeepL `#0f2b46`, Logos navy `#00042f`.
- Recent rebrands (Seed Company 2026 charcoal, illumiNations black, FCBH's new black app icon, Wycliffe USA's black wordmark) show a move toward **monochrome/ink** marks. Pure black is therefore becoming a shared look, not an open one.

### Gaps
- Pioneer Bible Translators, LBT, Biblica and Bible Brain block or refuse automated fetches. Their colours come only from favicons (approx.), not from CSS or guidelines.
- I found no announcements or design write-ups for the Wycliffe USA 2025, Seed Company 2026 or Paratext 2026 identity changes. The dates come only from asset file paths.
- I found no Brand New (UnderConsideration) review of SIL Global. The URL tried returned 404, and searches found none. Since the logo appears unchanged, there may be no review to find.
- I could not establish ETEN's official logo colours beyond the favicon and the most-used CSS colours.
- Scripture App Builder has no single brand icon; each app built with it carries its own.

## 2. Which organisations publish brand guidelines?

### Takeaway
Among the named organisations, only Duolingo publishes an open, detailed colour system. SIL Global offers logo downloads but no colour values or rules. I found no public brand guidelines for Wycliffe (any branch), unfoldingWord, YouVersion, FCBH, BibleProject or the Bible Societies. Their colours are only recoverable from site code and assets.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo publishes its colour system publicly, including Feather Green `#58CC02` and Macaw `#1CB0F6` — [design.duolingo.com/identity/color](https://design.duolingo.com/identity/color)
- SIL Global's Media Center offers master, black and white logo downloads. It gives no hex, RGB, CMYK or Pantone values and no guidelines document. The only rule is to contact info_sil@sil.org — [global.sil.org/media-center](https://global.sil.org/media-center/)
- Jesus Film Project published a rebrand announcement (April 2023) describing the logo concept, but it gives no colour values — [jesusfilm.org/?p=7264](https://www.jesusfilm.org/?p=7264)
- A search for YouVersion brand guidelines or a press kit found nothing official. The only result was a designer's portfolio mentioning a 2021 rebrand and a "YouVersion Red" — [Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/designbyremedy/projects/5305519-YouVersion?page=1)
- A search for unfoldingWord brand guidelines returned only unrelated organisations' guides — [search result example: Bibles for America style guide](https://biblesforamerica.org/styleguide/img/styleguide.pdf) (not unfoldingWord)
- Sociedad Bíblica Colombiana's site uses an image named `MANUAL-DE-MARCA_LOGO.jpg` (2025/12) as its share image. This hints at a brand manual, but I found no public manual — [sociedadbiblicacolombiana.org](https://sociedadbiblicacolombiana.org/)

### Inferences
- Because most sector organisations don't publish guidelines, the CSS-variable values in section 1 are the best available "official" hex values.

### Gaps
- I did not check DeepL's press kit or Google's brand-resources pages. The DeepL and Google Translate values come from live site code.
- I did not find whether Wycliffe UK, Wycliffe USA or UBS have partner-facing (non-public) brand books.

## 3. Which colour families dominate the sector and which are rare?

### Takeaway
Blue/navy/cyan (about 33%) and red/crimson (about 26%) dominate primary brand colours, and orange/gold is the default accent. Lime green is a growing "translation tools" cue. Truly open as a *primary* in this sector: **deep forest/bottle green, plum/aubergine, terracotta/brick**, and to a lesser degree warm violet. Deep teal and ink black are now partly taken (Wycliffe USA 2025; Seed Company 2026, illumiNations).

### Inferences
- **Crowded:** mid and royal blue, navy / dark blue-teal (the Servos ink range), bright red / crimson, orange-gold accents, cyan-on-white, lime green for tools.
- **Open as primary:** (1) **deep forest / bottle green** (no organisation uses it as primary; Wycliffe GA's logo green is only half of a two-colour mark); (2) **plum / aubergine / wine** (FCBH uses `#852f6f` only as an accent; COMIBAM's dark purple is the nearest and is LatAm-visible); (3) **terracotta / brick as primary** (only used as accents); (4) a **two-colour pairing that avoids blue + orange**. Blue + orange is the near-universal default (SIL, unfoldingWord, Wycliffe USA, Sociedad Bíblica Colombiana, UBS presets).
- Hallow occupies bright violet (`#6d0ec1`) in the Christian app space. A plum would need to be clearly darker or redder than that to avoid looking like Hallow.
- Terracotta sits next to the many reds (YouVersion, Bible.is, Jesus Film). At icon size it may read as "another red Bible app" unless it is clearly browner or more muted.
- Lime is now the shared cue of translation-workflow tools (Paratext 2026, ETEN, Door43). Using it would associate Servos with that tool family, but not distinguish it.

### Gaps

## 4. Which hues are most common among app icons overall, and which stand out on a typical Android home screen?

### Takeaway
Across app-icon studies, **blue** is the most common dominant colour, followed by red. **White** backgrounds became dominant in the top charts from about 2022, and black has grown with social and video apps. On a typical home screen the big neighbours are bright green (WhatsApp, Spotify, Duolingo), blue (Facebook, Messenger, Telegram, Translate), red (YouTube, Netflix, Bible apps), white-background Google icons, black (TikTok, Netflix, Spotify) and yellow (Mercado Libre). A full-bleed, mid-dark, *desaturated* warm or deep hue stands out more than another saturated primary or a white tile. On Android 13+ the shape and silhouette also matter, because themed icons can override colour.

### Cited Findings
- MindSea (2017, 300+ apps): blue was the most popular icon colour across all categories; red led Food & Drink — [MindSea colour theory](http://mindsea.com/blog/color-theory/)
- A later summary of MindSea's research reported blue as the primary colour of 23% of top apps, then red and white at 13% each. Red dominated 30% of the top 50 Food & Drink icons. (I saw this in a search summary, not on the source page.) — [MindSea app icon research](https://mindsea.com/app-icon-research/)
- Statista (Feb 2022, top iOS apps by category): **white** was the predominant icon colour across all categories combined. Gaming was mostly black, Food & Drink red and Finance dark blue. Exact percentages are paywalled — [Statista iOS](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1296126/icons-colors-top-ios-apps-by-category); Android equivalent: [Statista Android](https://statista.com/statistics/1296566/icons-colors-top-android-apps-by-category)
- Appbot (Sept 2022, top 200 free/paid/grossing on iOS and Google Play): many white icons; black is rising (TikTok, BeReal). Red, green and blue are spread fairly evenly; blue, red and yellow are the most popular colours. **Pink is rarely used**. The trend toward white and black has continued since 2015 — [Appbot "Colors of an app icon – 2022" (archived)](https://gwern.net/doc/www/appbot.co/63e0aefb174b5a7c489f539fd60bd0a6aa0310ed.html)
- AppTweak (Dec 2023, top 50 US apps per category): in Finance about 40% use blue as the primary colour, with green next; Travel 40–45% blue; Shopping 36–38% red/orange; Social mostly blue/purple, with 12–16% black — [AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/infographic-mobile-games-app-icon-trends-2)
- Jared Sinclair (2014, about 100 top apps): blue led, with white catching up — [jaredsinclair.com](https://jaredsinclair.com/2014/08/08/whats-the-most-common-app-icon-c.html)
- Measured icons of common home-screen neighbours (approx., from official store artwork):
  - WhatsApp: green `#25d366` full-bleed — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.whatsapp)
  - Facebook: blue `#0865fe` — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/facebook/id284882215)
  - Messenger: blue `#0361fc` on white — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/messenger/id454638411)
  - Telegram: cyan `#00ace4` — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/telegram-messenger/id686449807)
  - YouTube: red `#fe0032` on white — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/youtube/id544007664)
  - Netflix: black with red `#aa0b13` — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/netflix/id363590051)
  - TikTok: black — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tiktok-videos-shop-live/id835599320)
  - Spotify: black with green `#02e158` — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/spotify-music-and-podcasts/id324684580)
  - Instagram: orange-magenta gradient — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/instagram/id389801252)
  - Gmail, Chrome, Google Maps: multicolour on white — [Gmail](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gmail-email-by-google/id422689480); [Chrome](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/google-chrome/id535886823); [Maps](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/google-maps/id585027354)

- **Android adaptive icons:** two layers (foreground and background) of 108×108 dp. Only the inner 66×66 dp is guaranteed visible, and the OEM mask can be a circle or a squircle. **Themed icons (Android 13+)** tint a supplied `monochrome` layer with the user's wallpaper and theme colours. From **Android 16 QPR 2** the system auto-themes apps that don't provide a monochrome layer — [Android Developers: adaptive icons](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/launch/icon_design_adaptive)

### Inferences
- On a Latin American home screen dominated by WhatsApp green, Facebook/Messenger blue, YouTube red and white Google tiles, the least-used tile treatments are: full-bleed **deep green** (it differs from WhatsApp and Duolingo in *value*, not just hue), **plum / aubergine**, **terracotta / brick**, and **dark ink with one warm accent**. Pink and purple are rare overall (Appbot), but Hallow already uses bright violet in the faith niche.
- A white-background icon (like BibleProject, DeepL, Keyman, Bloom) blends into the many white Google tiles. A full-bleed coloured background is more findable.
- Because Android themed icons (Android 13+, automatic from 16 QPR 2) can replace the brand colour with a wallpaper tint, the Servos mark needs a strong silhouette that works as a single-colour glyph inside the 66 dp safe zone. Colour alone cannot carry recognition.
- Value contrast matters as much as hue. A mid-dark muted tone (roughly 25–40% lightness) next to the many saturated, bright icons is likely to read as distinct and calm. This is a design inference, not a measured result.

### Gaps
- I found no icon-colour study specific to Latin America, to low-end Android devices, or to faith apps. I also found no recent (2024–2026) cross-category percentage study; the 2022–2023 studies are the latest found.
- Statista's exact percentages are paywalled.
- I did not verify which apps are most installed in Latin America. The neighbour set above is an assumption based on globally dominant apps plus Mercado Libre.

## 5. Colours of Latin American indigenous-language or church organisations the audience would recognise

### Takeaway
The church and Bible bodies most visible in Latin America lean **blue** (SBB, SIL Mexico/Peru legacy blue, Sociedad Bíblica Colombiana cyan) and **red** (Spanish-language Bible Society dove-and-book, Wycliffe-family reds). COMIBAM, the Ibero-American missions network, is the notable **purple** (`#391753`). The traditional Bible Society "open book + dove" mark is the most recognisable sector symbol, and it appears in red or white.

### Cited Findings
- Sociedade Bíblica do Brasil: blue `#1298de` (manifest), and its Bíblia SBB app uses a blue gradient — [sbb.org.br](https://www.sbb.org.br/); [App Store BR](https://apps.apple.com/br/app/b%C3%ADblia-sbb/id1502745081)
- Sociedad Bíblica Colombiana (site from 2025): primary cyan `#20BCE1`, accent amber `#F9A11B`, navy `#1B2B5E`, cream `#F0E8D4` (Elementor globals). Its logo is a dove and an open book — [sociedadbiblicacolombiana.org](https://sociedadbiblicacolombiana.org/); [logo](https://sociedadbiblicacolombiana.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Logo-1-300x204.png)
- Sociedad Bíblica (Spain, Madrid) uses a red, approx. `#d10123`, open book with a dove — [sociedadbiblica.org](https://www.sociedadbiblica.org/)
- COMIBAM (Cooperación Misionera Iberoamericana): logo SVG fills `#391753` (dark purple, 72 occurrences) and `#0080c0` blue — [comibam.org](https://comibam.org/es/); [logo SVG](https://comibam.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/comibam-logo3.svg)
- SIL Mexico: the site's most-used colour is `#005cb9` (SIL blue), plus `#004386`, and its favicon is the legacy blue "SIL" square — [mexico.sil.org](https://mexico.sil.org/)
- SIL Peru: the site's most-used colour is maroon `#800000` (`#4d0000` dark variant), with the same SIL blue favicon — [peru.sil.org](https://peru.sil.org/)
- UBS's 2026 (80th anniversary) logo is a multicolour mosaic book — [unitedbiblesocieties.org](https://unitedbiblesocieties.org/)

### Inferences
- Blue and red would make Servos look "like a Bible Society or SIL product" to Latin American church audiences. That gives credibility, but not distinctiveness. COMIBAM's purple shows that a dark purple already has some LatAm missions association.
- The Bible Society dove-and-book is a strongly recognised symbol. Servos's "book that speaks" mark (commit `4fcbb4f`) shares the book idea, so a distinct colour helps keep it from reading as a Bible Society derivative.

### Gaps
- I could not reach the Sociedades Bíblicas Unidas Americas sites or the Mexican and Argentine Bible Society sites (domains guessed: sbu.org, biblia.org.ar, sociedadbiblica.org.mx — fetches failed). Their colours are unverified.
- I found no sourced material on colour symbolism in Latin American indigenous textiles or flags (for example the Andean wiphala, or Maya and Mixtec textile palettes) as it applies to branding for indigenous-language audiences. A regional source or consultation with the translation teams would be needed.
- I did not survey indigenous-language organisations' own branding (for example regional translation associations or mother-tongue networks in Mexico, Guatemala, Peru and Bolivia).
