The first step of each book is **Study**: understand the passage before writing it in another language. In the book, tap the **Study** step and then **Start** (or **Continue**, where you left off).

## The introductions

The study begins with the **Introduction to the book** and the introduction to each chapter: what it is about, who wrote it, and what may be hard to translate. Move from one section to the next with **‹** and **›**, at the bottom.

## Each section

Then comes the book section by section, a few verses at a time. At the top you choose what to read:

- **TPL**, the Literal Bridge Text (*Texto Puente Literal*): it follows the original closely.
- **TPS**, the Simple Bridge Text (*Texto Puente Simple*): it gives the meaning in simple words.
- **Hebrew** or **Greek**: the original text.
- **My note**: what you write about this section.

![What a word says](estudio-palabra.webp "When you tap a word: the original and its notes")

**Tap a word** in the text to see which word of the original it comes from, how the TPS says it, and the notes that talk about it. Under each verse are the **Notes on the whole verse**, and at the end of the section, **All the notes and key words** of those verses.

## The questions and your note

At the end of each section are the **Comprehension notes**: questions to check that the passage was understood (what does it say?, who speaks?, to whom?, what will be hard?) and the translation questions, with their answers under **See the answers**.

Tap **Write my note** to write down what you understood or what the team should talk about. **What you write will be seen later when translating** those verses.

> The percentage of the Study step counts the verses already studied. You do not need to reach one hundred percent to start translating: the team decides when to go on with the next step, and it can come back to this one whenever it wants.
