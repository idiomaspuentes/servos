resumen: The notes, the key words, the passage with four questions, and the alignment with the original.

In the **Checking** step there are four tools. Each one shows **one thing at a time** and keeps count of what has been checked, verse by verse. You can use them in the order the team prefers, and several people can check on their own devices.

![The tools of the Checking step](revision.webp "The Checking step")

## Check with the notes

It makes sure that each hard phrase, the ones that have a translation note, was translated as its note explains. Each note is checked in **four steps**:

1. **Read**: the verse in the TPL or the TPS, with the phrase highlighted.
2. **Understand**: the note and, if needed, its Translation Academy article.
3. **Mark**: tap the words in your translation that say that phrase.
4. **Confirm**: whether they say the meaning well. If not, you can improve it right away in the editor or leave it noted for later.

![The first step: read](revisar-leer.webp "1. Read the verse with the phrase highlighted")
![The second step: understand the note](revisar-entender.webp "2. Understand the note")

At the top, **All the notes** lets you go by category (for example, figures of speech) or verse by verse.

## Check with the words

The same, for each use of a key word, of other terms and of names. Before going on, you read the word's whole article, to see all its meanings, and at the end you compare how it was translated in the other places in the book: the same where it means the same, different where it means something else.

## Check the passage

You read each whole section and weigh it with four questions, one at a time:

- **Faithful**: does it say the same as the source text?
- **Clear**: is it understood the first time?
- **Natural**: does it sound the way people speak?
- **Precise**: are the details right? (names, numbers, places, key words)

If there is something to improve, mark the words with your finger and open the editor, or leave it noted: the note shows under its verse in the editor until someone marks it as improved.

## Align the translation

It links each word of your translation with the word of the source text it translates: the TPL, the TPS or the original. Tap a word (or several) and then the box of the word it translates. The alignment goes with the book when you publish it on Door43.
