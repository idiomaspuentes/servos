## Your language and your name

When you open Servos for the first time, choose the app's language (Spanish, Portuguese or English) and write your name. Your name stays on everything you do, so the team knows who wrote what. Tap **Start**.

![The first screen: the app's language and your name](bienvenida.webp "The first screen")

There is no password and no account. If other people will use the same phone or the same computer, you add them later, in the menu with your name.

Do you just want to get to know the app without touching anything? On the first screen there is **See the example as Ana**: a team translating Jonah, Titus and Ruth.

## The texts and helps

**On the phone and on the computer**, Servos carries inside the Spanish bridge texts (the Literal and the Simple), their helps (notes, questions, key words and the Translation Academy) and the Hebrew and Greek with their lexicons. The first time, it gets them ready by itself: on the home screen you read **Getting ready the texts and helps that come with the app…** with how far it has gone. On a phone it takes a few minutes; on a computer, a few seconds. Meanwhile you can go on with the project.

## Receive the texts and helps

You need this **only in the browser**, or when a newer pack or a pack in another language arrives. The packs are files whose names end in **.langpack.zip**:

- **originals.langpack.zip**: the Hebrew and Greek, with their lexicons.
- **es-419.langpack.zip**: the Spanish bridge texts and their helps.

1. Tap your name, at the top right, and choose **Receive texts and helps**.
2. Tap **Choose the file** and look for **originals.langpack.zip** on the memory card, the USB stick, or the Bluetooth or Xender folder.
3. Servos tells you what the pack brings. Tap **Open the pack** and wait for **Done**.
4. Do the same with **es-419.langpack.zip**.

![The menu with your name](menu.webp "The menu with your name")
![What the Spanish pack brings](paquete-es.webp "Before opening it, Servos tells you what it brings")
![The pack is now on the phone](paquete-listo.webp "Done: now it can be read without internet")

A pack made for an earlier version of Servos no longer opens: use the ones that come with your version, or ask the person who coordinates for the new ones.

> **Which books are ready.** With all the helps in Spanish: **Jonah, Ruth, Titus and 3 John**. **Esther and Nehemiah** have only the Literal Bridge Text for now. When there are more, they will come over the internet, in a new version of the app or in a new pack, which you receive the same way.

## With internet

Servos does not need internet to work, but when there is internet, it makes use of it:

- **New texts and helps.** If there is something new for the project's bridge language, the home screen says so, with its size: **There are new texts and helps for Latin American Spanish (680 KB)**. Tap **Download**, or **Not now**. While it downloads, you can keep working.
- **A book that is not on the device.** When you add it, Servos tells you how much it will download (**Creating it will download its texts and helps (7.9 MB)**) and downloads it by itself.
- **Another bridge language.** When you create the project, in **More options › Bridge texts and helps**, tap **Another language…** and write its name. Servos asks the server and tells you what it has, for example **English (en) · On the server · 66 books**.

What was downloaded stays on the device and is used without internet, like what came inside the app.

## Add the team's project

A project is the translation into one language. The person who coordinates creates it once and shares it with the others (**Settings › Share the project**, see [Bring the work together](/docs/juntar/)); the others receive it with **Add a project › Open a project someone sent you**, instead of creating another one.

1. On the home screen, tap **Add a project** and choose **New Bible**.
2. In **Language**, write the name of the people's language or its code, and choose it from the list. If it is not there, tap **Not in the list: write it by hand**.
3. The project's name fills in by itself; you can change it.
4. In **More options** are the text's direction, the font and the **bridge texts and helps** the team will work with. If you do not change them, the ones on the device stay (in Spanish).
5. Choose **who can see this team's work**. With **Only the team**, the files you share carry a password and do not say what they are. With **Anyone who has the file**, they open without a password. If you are not sure, leave **Only the team**.
6. Tap **Create the Bible**.

## Add a book

1. When you create the Bible, Servos right away offers the first book: tap **Start a blank book**. (If you closed that step, on the home screen tap **Add a book →**.)
2. At the top are the books **ready on this phone**, the ones that came in the pack. Choose one, for example **Jonah** or **Ruth**. With internet you can also choose one that is not there: Servos downloads it when you create it (see [With internet](#with-internet)).
3. Tap **Create Jonah** (or the book you chose).

![Choose the book](elegir-libro.webp "At the top, the books that have texts and helps")
![The book and its five steps](libro.webp "At the top, the last thing you did; below, the steps")

The book opens at **Pick up where you left off**, with the button to go back to the last thing you did in it. Below are its five steps, each with how much is done: **Study**, **Translation**, **Checking**, **Reading with the community** and **Publishing**. Tap a step to see its tools; with **back** you return to the book. The steps do not close: the team can go back to any of them whenever it wants.

Servos also asks **how to say the book's name** in the people's language: that is how it appears in the app and when you publish. You can write it now or later, in **More › Book name**.

If the book is already started in another app (Paratext, translationCore), choose **From a USFM file** instead of a blank book.

## The home screen

When you open Servos, the project's name is at the top and, in the green box, the book you worked on last time: what you were doing, how far it has gone, and the button to go on where you left off. To see that book's steps, tap its name.

Below are the other books, each with its step, and **+ Add a book**. If the project has many books, you see the three you opened last and **See all 12 books** (with whatever the number is), which shows them all. Then comes the **Project glossary**, with how many words it has: it opens from here, without going into a book. At the bottom, the project's **Settings** and **+ Another project**.

If something you asked to send to Door43 is still waiting for internet, the home screen says so in one line, with **Review and send** once there is internet.

If the team has more than one project, tap the project's name, at the top, to move to another one. On the computer, the projects are in a column on the left.

## Archive or delete a project

A project you no longer use can be **archived**: it leaves the home screen, but all its work stays on the device.

1. On the home screen, tap the project's **Settings** and, at the end, **Archive this project**.
2. Servos asks first. Tap **Yes, archive it**.

Archived projects are in the menu with your name, under **Archived projects**. There each one has two buttons:

- **Bring back**: it returns to the home screen, as it was.
- **Delete for good**: Servos asks again and tells you what is deleted and what stays. All its work is deleted from the device: the books, the recordings, the glossary and what was waiting for internet. **It cannot be undone.** What was already uploaded to Door43 and the copy on the memory card are not deleted.

> Before you delete a project, share its books if someone else needs them (see [Bring the work together](/docs/juntar/)).
